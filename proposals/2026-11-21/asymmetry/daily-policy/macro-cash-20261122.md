# Daily Macro Review: Cash >=70% Hypothesis

**As of:** Sunday, 2026-11-22; latest completed trading session: 2026-11-20. Research only; no forecast, numeric scenario odds, code changes, or GitHub operations.

**Bottom line:** Weak final-demand indicators, limited broad fiscal spending growth, and upstream cost pressure make a defensive cash position defensible for a drawdown-sensitive investor. They do not establish that >=70% cash is optimal. October fiscal flows and the retrieved AAA credit spreads supply material counterevidence to an inevitable, linear deterioration narrative.

Scope: The previously verified NBS November 16 activity data, PBOC November 13 government-supported credit, SSE week-ending November 20 technology decline, and Fed 3.75%-4.00% range are inherited context, not rechecked here. The owner's allocation assertion is a hypothesis, not independent evidence. Browser skill was read; browser/delegation tools were unavailable, so primary pages and their published read-only data endpoints were retrieved directly.

## 2. Actual Payments Versus Issuance

The November 20 release reports **budget expenditure**, an execution measure beyond announcing or issuing bonds. It does **not** identify how much October financing reached final suppliers, cleared arrears in cash, financed new work, or replaced existing obligations.

MOF's October 9 first-half execution report repeatedly describes funds as allocated/issued to budgets and projects ("xiada"), including CNY 9.4 trillion of transfers allocated to local governments. Allocation is not proof of final-recipient cash receipt. Even its invoice-lottery prize figure explicitly includes prizes awaiting redemption. [F6]

The State Council's September 3 notice explicitly distinguishes allocation from payment: government procurement must use cash; project owners and general contractors must transmit payments downstream; completed but unsettled projects should settle and pay. This demonstrates the policy's transmission concern, not measured November compliance. **No national October final-creditor cash-payment/arrears-clearance series was identified in these reviewed releases; this is a verification gap, not proof that payments were absent.** Debt replacement can relieve interest and cash-flow pressure without generating equivalent new expenditure. [F7]

## 3. Prices And Rates: Input-Cost Risk, Not Proven Broad Stagflation

NBS October price releases, published November 9: [P1, P2]

- CPI: +0.7% YoY, flat MoM; core CPI excluding food/energy: +0.8% YoY, flat MoM; services: +0.7% YoY.
- Producer output prices: +5.7% YoY, +0.7% MoM. Producer purchasing prices: +7.9% YoY, +1.1% MoM.
- Output prices for production materials: +7.4% YoY; consumer/living materials: -0.1%. Fuel/power purchasing prices: +18.6% YoY; chemical inputs: +16.8%.

October PMI, published October 31, corroborates the distinction: manufacturing new orders 49.4, input-price index 56.8, selling-price index 51.7; nonmanufacturing new orders 44.4, input prices 51.5, selling prices 49.5. Manufacturing input-price diffusion nevertheless eased from September's 60.8: costs are not uniformly accelerating on every measure. These are diffusion indexes, not inflation percentages. [P3]

**Interpretation:** The combination is consistent with weak final demand and upstream cost/pass-through risk, particularly for downstream firms without pricing power. It is not proof of economy-wide stagnation, generalized consumer inflation, a specific supply-shock cause, or falling margins at every company. Input and output indexes have different baskets; their 2.2-percentage-point YoY gap is not a measured margin change. Upstream producers and selected exporters may benefit.

The November 20 PBOC-authorized LPR announcement gives 1-year **3.00%** and 5-year-plus **3.50%**. ChinaBond's November 20 government yield curve gives 3-month **1.1744%**, 1-year **1.1753%**, and 10-year **1.5803%**. LPR is a lending benchmark; bond yields are modeled market YTMs, not deposit returns or guaranteed cash-fund yields. Neither contemporaneous CPI subtraction nor the inherited Fed range establishes an investor's forward real cash return. [R1, C1]

## 5. Competing Cases For Cash >=70%

**Strongest defensive case:** The already verified demand/market weakness is reinforced by nearly flat local general-budget expenditure, impaired land revenue, and higher upstream costs against weak consumer pricing power. Government-led financing need not become private demand. A drawdown-sensitive owner can rationally prioritize liquidity, avoid forced selling, and retain capacity to buy if valuations become attractive. This is a capital-preservation policy, not a demonstrated return-maximizing allocation.

**Strongest bullish alternative:** October fund-budget expenditure is already growing YoY; high-grade credit spreads did not widen in the retrieved week; core inflation remains low; and the previously verified industrial/export strength persists as relevant counterevidence. Fiscal transmission, easing input pressure, or policy credibility could reprice risk assets before backward-looking activity data improve. Staying predominantly in cash can incur substantial opportunity cost. A selective, staged exposure to demonstrable cash-flow/pricing-power beneficiaries is a plausible alternative, subject to valuation.

**Strongest bearish alternative:** Cash could be even more appropriate if payment transmission fails, downstream cash conversion deteriorates, and financial conditions tighten across weaker credits. Upstream commodity exposure and long-duration bonds are not automatic substitutes: demand reversal, policy intervention, or inflation/rate shocks can hurt them. None of those future developments is established by the current snapshot.

**Cannot infer:** An optimal 70% threshold, imminent market drawdown, adequate valuation compensation, generalized credit distress, or a particular company/fund's margin outcome. Allocation requires horizon, loss tolerance, liabilities, portfolio exposures, and cash currency/instrument. Foreign-currency cash carries FX risk; deposits and funds have distinct counterparty, liquidity, and reinvestment risks.

## 6. Forward Regime Framework For Daily Reviews

Keep a dated evidence ledger: **new release -> transmission channel -> competing interpretation -> portfolio condition that would change the view**. Hold monthly data unchanged between releases; weekends introduce no new closing price. Start with macro/financial conditions, then valuation and portfolio exposure, then the report's claims.

| Forward regime | Evidence to watch, without assuming the outcome | Conditional response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transmission improves / costs ease | Repeated spending-flow improvement, disclosed cash arrears payments and shorter receivable days, broader new orders, improved downstream cash flow, stable cross-rating spreads and market breadth | Reconsider the high-cash stance; evaluate staged risk addition against valuations |
| Weak demand / costly inputs persists | Orders stay weak, input pressure remains elevated relative to selling prices, payments lag, local execution remains subdued | Retain defensive liquidity; require company-level pass-through and cash conversion |
| Financial stress broadens | Verified lower-grade spread widening, worsening refinancing/payment conditions, funding strain and broader market weakness | Prioritize genuinely liquid high-quality short-duration assets in the liability currency; reassess concentration and counterparties |

Do not require every lagging series to recover before considering risk, or treat one rally/one weak release as a regime change. Define the evidence and action rules before observing the next outcome; do not retrofit the cash thesis to subsequent prices.
